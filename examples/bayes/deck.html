Introductory statistics · a spam filter example (fictional numbers)

Bayes' theorem

Updating a belief, one piece of evidence at a time

A mail says "free". Is it spam?

To answer, we need two things: how common spam is, and the word

Before reading: 1 in 5 mails is spam

Out of 1,000 mails in this (fictional) inbox

The prior: P(spam) = 0.2, before we look at any word

Count the mails that contain "free"

"Free" is in 40% of spam, but also in 10% of other mails

160 mails say "free", and 80 of them are spam: 1 in 2

The same count, written as a formula

\[ P(\text{spam}\mid\text{free}) = \frac{\htmlId{lik}{P(\text{free}\mid\text{spam})}\;\htmlId{pri}{P(\text{spam})}}{\htmlId{evi}{P(\text{free})}} \]
PartNameValue here
\(P(\text{free}\mid\text{spam})\)likelihood0.4
\(P(\text{spam})\)prior0.2
\(P(\text{free})\)evidence\(0.4 \times 0.2 + 0.1 \times 0.8 = 0.16\)

So \(P(\text{spam}\mid\text{free}) = 0.4 \times 0.2 \div 0.16 = \hlE{0.5}\)

Posterior = likelihood × prior ÷ evidence

One word at a time

Each posterior becomes the prior for the next word

Evidence moves the belief step by step: 20% → 50% → 95%

Prior, evidence, posterior

Word by word, as here, assumes the words appear independently (the "naive Bayes" filter)

Never judge the evidence alone: start from the prior
