A Theoretical Analysis of Bayes-Optimal Calibration
under the Logit-Noise Model

Noriaki Abe and Gendo Kumoi
Nagaoka University of Technology

Confident, but wrong

  • Modern classifiers are overconfident
  • Standard fix: temperature scaling
    \[ \hat p \;\mapsto\; \sigma\!\left(\logit(\hat p)/T\right) \]
  • It works. But why is one parameter enough?
We give a generative answer

The question

Under what generative model does temperature scaling
approximate the Bayes-optimal correction?

  • Not just a good heuristic, but an approximation to the optimal fix (squared error)
  • And if so, what determines \(T\)?
Answer: Gaussian logit noise, with a flat prior

The logit-noise model

\[ \htmlId{theta-star}{\theta^* = \logit(p^*)}, \qquad \htmlId{eps}{\hat\theta = \theta^* + \varepsilon,\ \ \varepsilon \sim \mathcal N(0,\tau^2)}, \qquad \htmlId{sig}{\hat p = \sigma(\hat\theta)} \]
The assumption everything builds on

Noise creates overconfidence

  • \(\tau = 0\): perfectly calibrated
  • Larger \(\tau\): extreme predictions are less trustworthy
\[ C(p) = \E\left[\,p^* \mid \hat p = p\,\right] \]
Logit noise naturally produces overconfidence

Calibration as an inverse problem

\[ \htmlId{C}{\morph{opt}{\tilde p^{\,\mathrm{opt}}(p) = \E\left[\,p^* \mid \hat p = p\,\right]}} \]
Optimal correction = posterior mean

Main theorem: a sigmoid averaged over the posterior

\[ \morph{opt}{\tilde p^{\,\mathrm{opt}}(p) = \E\left[\,p^* \mid \hat p = p\,\right]} \]
\[ = \htmlId{post}{\morph{avg}{\E_{Z\sim\mathcal N(\mu_{\mathrm{post}},\,\sigma^2_{\mathrm{post}})}\left[\sigma(Z)\right]}} \]

Gaussian prior \(\theta^*\sim\mathcal N(m,s^2)\): posterior in closed form

Noise + curvature = shrinkage

It is temperature scaling

\[ \morph{avg}{\E_{Z\sim\mathcal N(\mu_{\mathrm{post}},\,\sigma^2_{\mathrm{post}})}\left[\sigma(Z)\right]} \]
\[ \approx\ \sigma\!\left(\frac{\logit(\hat p)}{T}\right) \]
\[ \htmlId{T}{T = \sqrt{1 + \pi\tau^2/8}} \]

MacKay error ≤ 0.015 (for T ≤ 3)

Bayes-optimal (flat prior) ≈ temperature-scaling form

Gaussian prior: Platt scaling

\[ \tilde p \approx \sigma\!\left(A\,\logit(\hat p) + B\right) \]
\[ \htmlId{A}{A < 1/T < 1} \]
  • The slope is always below one: it always contracts
  • Temperature scaling is the flat-prior limit (\(B = 0\))
A built-in contraction (slope < 1)

A synthetic check

  • Before: points follow the predicted curve
  • After temperature scaling with the theoretical \(T\)
  • Fitted \(T\) within 1.4% of theory (all four \(\tau\))

\(\tau = 1.5\), 20,000 samples

Theory and practice agree when the model holds

Real learners bend the assumption

Four departures (residual diagnostics)

  1. Mean depends on \(\theta^*\)
  2. Variance depends on \(\theta^*\)
  3. Heavy tails (excess kurtosis +2.0)
  4. Rank dependence
The theory also tells us what to check

When it helps, when it breaks

  • Overlapping classes (var 1.0): lower calibration error
  • Well-separated classes: with the noise-derived \(T\), the error is about 10× worse at var 0.1
A noise-derived \(T \ge 1\) can only shrink, never stretch

Takeaways

  1. Under logit noise, the optimal correction is a sigmoid averaged over a Gaussian posterior
  2. Under a flat prior, its MacKay approximation has the temperature-scaling form, \(T=\sqrt{1+\pi\tau^2/8}\)
  3. The theory explains when a noise-derived \(T\) works and when it cannot

Binary classification; experiments assume the true logit is known. Estimating \(\tau\) from real data is future work.
