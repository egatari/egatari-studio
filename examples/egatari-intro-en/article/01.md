## Visuals narrate. You pass it on.

Egatari (picture-telling) is a format that puts the material you use to tell people something into one HTML file. It fits any situation where you take people through something step by step.

(sec-5bkyrg)=
## Figures build in the order you speak

The figures on a slide appear step by step, in the order you speak. In a report, for example, you can show the key numbers first and then the growth quarter by quarter as bars. Your audience only needs to look at what you are talking about right now. (The figures on the slide are fictional.)

(sec-hqg6hw)=
## Slides and a book, in one file

One file holds a face for showing (the slides) and a face for reading (the book). The text you are reading now is the book. People who missed the talk can read it later, at their own pace. Press r to switch between the slides and the book.

(sec-jd4hte)=
## The AI need not get it right the first time

A chat AI can write the slides and the script from a short brief. The instructions that make the slides work well are long and detailed, though, and an AI may not follow all of them the first time. So Egatari's quality check examines what the AI wrote straight away. It finds problems such as text that overflows or headings that repeat, and it writes what to fix and how, in words the AI can act on. You copy that fix request into the same chat and paste the AI's reply back into Egatari. The finding shown on the slide is a real one: the AI cut one line and passed after a single round.

(sec-anspnd)=
## Anywhere there is a browser

The result is one HTML file, with the player and the fonts inside it. It opens in the browser of a computer, tablet or phone, with no app to install. You can send it by email, put it on a course site, export it as a video, or run it live so that participants can ask questions and vote. Whoever receives it can open it and edit it.
