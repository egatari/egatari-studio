# Narration: Exponential decay

## Exponential decay
[0] Today we look at exponential decay, using radioactive atoms as the example.

## The decay law
[0] Here is the decay law. We follow how many atoms are left as time passes.
[1] The number left is the starting number times an exponential that shrinks over time.
[2] {mark:n0|circle}{N₀|N naught} is the number of atoms at the start, at time zero.
[3] {mark:lam}{λ|lambda} is the decay constant, the fraction lost each second.
[concl] The time to lose half depends only on the decay constant, not on how many atoms we start with.

## A larger λ decays faster
[0] Let us change the decay constant and watch the share of atoms left.
[1] Raising it from 0.2 to 0.5 makes the curve fall faster.
[2] At 1.0 it falls faster still.
[concl] A larger decay constant means a shorter half-life.
